# V136 Controlled Clarification-value Results

## Outcome

V136 passed every frozen value, safety, and access gate:

> `targeted_clarification_has_model_free_value_authorize_direct_vs_thinking_preregistration_only`

Across all five V135 ambiguity families and all three candidate-prior regimes, a perfect family-specific
clarification answer was worth its 0.30 cost. The same was true at 95% binary answer reliability. The worst
95%-reliability condition still reduced expected decision cost by 0.30.

The maximum reliability required for asking to become strictly better than the no-query Bayes decision was
91.3%. This is materially below V130's 97.25% boundary for a single complete eleven-way answer. The reduction
has a precise cause: V136's question is selected after the language has localized uncertainty to one known
candidate and one explicit alternative, so its answer discriminates two live hypotheses rather than solving
the entire open-world classification problem at once.

At 95% reliability, maximum false-known probability on the non-known right truth was 5%, and the minimum
probability of a safe non-known terminal decision was 95%. Every clear case preferred the exact no-query
decision over paying to ask. The value policy is therefore genuinely history-dependent in the controlled
setting: ask on registered ambiguity, skip on decisive language.

## Interpretation and boundary

This does not show that Qwen, another model, or a person supplies a 95%-reliable clarification answer. It
shows that the frozen targeted interface has enough decision value to justify measuring realizability. The
terminal outputs are shadow typed decisions only; declaring a valid undeclared choice neither registers nor
executes a capability.

V134 language remained unopened. No external language, model, API, training, action, side effect, or
execution was used. Passing authorizes only prospective preregistration of direct and thinking-enabled local
model conditions on the frozen V135 synthetic development asset. It does not yet authorize those model
generations, V134 access, APIs, induction, training, authority, action, or execution.
